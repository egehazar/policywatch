## What it means
- On the 7 documents with 10 or more known positives (cases whose approved quote is still found in the document's
  current text), Kev reading only the reranker's top 3 passages per case ranked those cases above the frequent cases the
  service has no point for better than the reranker reading every passage: .771 against .693, +.078 [+.034, +.121].
  The difference falls inside the predicted range (+.02 to +.10).
- Both arms came in above their predicted ranges (CASCADE_KEV .66-.76, RERANK .58-.68), the reranker too, though it
  uses no shortlist (.693 here, .648 in the larger test, .675 in the pilot). So these documents, or this proxy on them,
  were easier than I expected; it does not show that the shortlist lost less than I expected.
- This is the third missing-points measurement with Kev ahead of the reranker (the pilot +.084 and the larger test
  +.071, both with Kev on every passage; here +.078 with Kev on the shortlist only). It is the cascade arm, so it is not
  a plain replication, and it uses the same proxy as the tests: cases already approved on the document stand in for
  "the document states the case".
- Kev is higher on 6 of the 7 documents, but two of those are near ties (Twitch +.005, X +.025); the gain comes from
  Reddit's privacy policy (+.15), Spotify's terms (+.21), YouTube's privacy policy (+.12) and Snapchat's terms (+.10).
  The exception is Facebook's privacy policy (-.06: .699 against .763), the longest document in the run (146
  passages). My guess was that with 3 of 146 passages per case the shortlist misses more there. An exploratory run of
  Kev on every passage of that document (6,862 cells; `stale/missing_wide_full.py`, its question committed before it
  ran) says no: Kev on every passage did worse, .656. Reading all 146 passages lifted the unapproved cases' best score
  more than the approved ones' (mean .528 to .692, against .721 to .805), though 11 of the 19 known positives found
  their best passage outside the shortlist. Recomputed after the fact from the same scores, a shortlist of 1, 5, 10,
  20 or 50 passages gives .662-.671; 3 gives .699. On this document the reranker ranked better at any width. One
  document, no interval (`stale/out/missing_wide_full.json`).
- The base family (Qwen3.5-4B) was not run here. In the larger test (and in the relocation tests) it showed no
  difference from Kev, so this is probably a result about asking a language model each question on a passage rather
  than about decision-model training; this run does not show either.

## What it does not show
- No suggestion from this run was rated, by a person or by an AI. The page gives the larger test's rated precision for
  the cascade (73 of its 100 top-10 suggestions rated YES by an AI rater) as the closest measure. These documents are
  longer and differ from the larger test's, so that figure may not carry over.
- The measure ranks cases ToS;DR already approved here against cases it has no point for on the service. The page lists
  the second kind. A high AUROC says Kev's scores separate the two; it does not say a given suggestion is right.
- Not every document of the 20 platforms. The rule took documents from `stale/out/reloc_docs.json`, which lists only
  documents with at least one approved ToS;DR point matched to them, so 6 of the 40 privacy policies and terms in the
  archive were left out: BeReal's privacy policy and terms, Mastodon's, Telegram's and TikTok's terms, and Twitch's
  privacy policy. With these 20, the page covered 34 of the 40. The 6 were run afterwards through the same cascade
  (`stale/missing_rest.py`, 2026-11-17); with no matched point there is nothing to check them against, so their
  60 suggestions are on the page marked not rated, and the page covers all 40.
- Telegram's privacy policy in the archive is in German (and so are its terms, added later); its suggestions quote
  German passages, and the page says so on both. No policywatch test checked Kev on German text.
- On the documents with the most known positives (Reddit's and YouTube's privacy policies, X's terms) few frequent
  cases are left unapproved: the tenth suggestion's score is .38-.53, the three lowest of the 20 documents, and X's
  9th and 10th (.498, .385) and YouTube's 10th (.488) are below .5, where Kev leans "no".

## Cost
- The reranker took 5.9 of the 8.1 h. Kev's 2.31 s per asked cell is about twice the larger test's 1.11 s. The likely
  reason, not tested directly: each request here carried about 5 questions on average (2 to 36 per document) instead of
  45-83, so each passage's prefill was shared by fewer questions. The run's own documents fit that: Mastodon's privacy
  policy, at 36 questions per request, ran at 1.10 s per asked cell, and Facebook's, at about 1.7 asked questions per
  request, at 4.47 s; the passages are about as long as the larger test's (1,281 against 1,257 characters on average).
- The cascade still saved about 10 h against Kev on every cell (estimated, not run). The Facebook run above is the one
  measured case: 18.6 h for one document. The first 70 passages took 16.2 h, all but their last minutes with the
  scanner backfill sharing the server; the other 76 took 2.4 h after it was paused (other page jobs ran alongside both).
- A cheaper first filter in front of the reranker (BM25 takes milliseconds) would not cut much without changing the
  shortlist (exploratory, from the run's own scores, `stale/bm25_prefilter.py`): letting the reranker see only BM25's
  top 20 passages per case needs 40% of its cells, but changes 37% of the shortlists and the passage behind 23 of the
  page's 200 suggestions; top 30 needs 58% and still changes 18% (10 of the 200). Case titles are short paraphrases,
  and the words of the legal text often differ. A changed shortlist is not shown to be a worse one; Kev was not run
  on the new passages.
