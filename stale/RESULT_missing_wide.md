## What it means
- On the 7 documents where ToS;DR already has 10 or more approved points, Kev reading only the reranker's top 3
  passages per case ranked those approved cases above the frequent cases the service has no point for better than the
  reranker reading every passage: .771 against .693, +.078 [+.034, +.121]. The difference falls inside the predicted
  range (+.02 to +.10). Both arms came in above their predicted ranges (CASCADE_KEV .66-.76, RERANK .58-.68): I
  expected the longer documents to cost the shortlist more than they did.
- This is the third missing-points measurement with Kev ahead of the reranker (the pilot +.08 and the larger test +.071,
  both with Kev on every passage; here +.078 with Kev on the shortlist only). It is the cascade arm, so it is not a plain
  replication, and it uses the same proxy as the tests: cases already approved on the document stand in for "the
  document states the case".
- Kev is higher on 6 of the 7 documents. The exception is Facebook's privacy policy (.699 against .763), the longest
  document in the run (146 passages). One guess, not tested: with 3 of 146 passages per case, the shortlist misses
  more there. Running Kev on every passage of that one document (6,862 cells, about 2 h) would test it.
- The base family (Qwen3.5-4B) was not run here. In both tests it showed no difference from Kev, so this is a
  result about asking a language model each question on a passage, not about decision-model training.
