  states this figure for these documents instead of borrowing the larger test's.
- Kev on the shortlist beat the reranker's own top 10 again, on 28 documents neither earlier test used: +.14
  [+.075, +.21], inside the predicted range. The page's method was ahead on 18 documents, tied on 7 and behind on 3
  (BeReal's two by one card each, Parler's privacy policy by two).
- Facebook's privacy policy is the one document where the reranker ranked approved cases better (the run's check,
  AUROC .763 against .699); here, on new suggestions, the page's method had 7 of 10 rated YES and the reranker 2.
  The proxy (approved cases standing in for "stated") and the rated suggestions do not always agree.
- The page is weakest where ToS;DR has already done the work. On the 9 documents with 19 or more cases already
  approved on them, 45 of 90 suggestions were rated YES; on the other 19, 145 of 190. X's terms (53 approved, 2 of 10)
  and YouTube's privacy policy (42 approved, 3 of 10) are the lowest. Across the 28, the YES count falls as approved
  cases rise (Spearman -.56; exploratory, `stale/wide_rate_explore.py`).
- Rank matters: the top 5 per document were rated YES 77% of the time, ranks 6-10 59%.
- Kev's printed p(yes) sorts them well (exploratory; bins chosen after the overall result was known): 144 of the 170
  suggestions at .90 or above were rated YES (85%), 44 of 96 between .70 and .90, 2 of 14 below .70. A curator
  could read the .90s first.
- Telegram's two German documents did not stand out (7 and 6 of 10); without them the difference is the same
  (+.14 [+.069, +.20]).

## What it does not show
- These are AI raters (two Claude Opus subagents, one per half of the cards), not people and not ToS;DR's curators.
  The larger test's 73 of 100 was measured the same way, so the two figures compare; neither says what a curator would
  accept. YES or PARTLY covers 94% of the page's cards, so most of the rest are near misses rather than nonsense.
- A YES says the shown passage states the case. It does not say ToS;DR lacks the point: its public data shows only
  approved points, so some suggestions may already be pending or declined there, and some are variants of a case
  already approved for the service (the larger test found 6 variants and 20 close ones among its 69 YES; not checked
  here).
- The two raters split the cards, so each saw different cards; their PAGE shares are close (.67, .69), their RERANK
  shares less so (.49, .58). Not a measure of agreement; GPT's ratings below are the second opinion.
