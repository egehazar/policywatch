Which approved ToS;DR points lost their evidence, and where is the case stated now?

ToS;DR’s volunteer curators back every point (“You waive your right to a class action”) with a quote from a service’s terms. Open Terms Archive records every version of those terms. policywatch checks every approved quote against every version, lists the points whose quote disappeared in a platform’s own edit, and suggests where the case may be stated now. It runs on a desktop CPU; every test below was written down and hashed before it was scored, failures included.

How one stale point is handled
  1. Find the drop. String matching of the quote in every archived version gives the commit where it left, and the passage that replaced it.
  2. Check ToS;DR’s copy. Does its stored document still hold the quote? Then a re-crawl is pending and the point will break.
  3. Rank the current text. A 4B model is asked, of each 1,500-character passage, whether it states the case. When the top passage leads the next by a wide margin, the suggestion is marked Clear.
  4. Propose a quote. Within the top passage, the sentence the model rates highest, highlighted for the curator to check.

Where the decision model pays

Cost: Kev reads a passage once, however many questions it answers

Seconds per passage-question, measured on an idle 6-core CPU (60 passages, each read once per model). Kev costs 7.4 s to read a passage plus 0.93 s per question (7.0 s and 0.95 s without five early calls slowed by other work); the instruct model 6.3 s for every passage-question, later questions no cheaper than the first.

With one question per passage Kev is the slower one (0.83x); it is 1.4 times cheaper than its base family at 2 questions, 2.4x at 4, 3.4x at 8 and 4.7x at 16. The reranker is cheaper still, and letting it shortlist 10 passages per case for Kev lost at most about 2 points (63% against 62.5%); Kev still reads about half the passages, so the estimated saving is about 38% of the time.

Limits
  • The audits and the “states the case” ratings are Claude models judging blind, not people. A rater that shares a language model’s sense of relevance could favour language-model picks; a rater from another company (GPT) reached the same conclusions, which narrows that risk without removing it.
  • Suggestions are a place to look, not a verdict: nothing here decides whether a point still holds.
  • The daily watch has run twice by hand and is not scheduled.
